# Catch.Release — Lore and Writing Guide

This document defines what Catch.Release is about, how its setting works, what its characters know, and how prose for the mod should sound.

Read it before writing dialogue, `rules.csv` text, fish descriptions, tackle blurbs, intel entries, colony descriptions, tooltips, mission text, or UI copy.

The writing is load-bearing. Catch.Release does not explain its premise through a codex or a reveal. The premise exists in the accumulated language of ordinary interactions. One line that treats the wrong thing as extraordinary can do more damage than a page of exposition can repair.

When this document conflicts with older Catch.Release prose, this document wins.

For rules-engine behavior, use the separate Starsector rules references. This document governs **fiction, language, characterization, terminology, and information release**, not CSV execution semantics.

---

# 1. The premise

**On the surface, Catch.Release is a whimsical fishing mod. Underneath it, there is nothing good.**

Both readings must remain true at the same time.

The surface reading is the one the game states.

You fish. You buy tackle. You trade catch. Children want fish for a tournament. A cook needs three different specimens for a plate. A cheerful trader sells equipment from a coat with too many pockets. Patrol officers fine you for firing an industrial fishing harpoon through somebody's hull.

All of this is allowed to be funny.

A player who engages only with that layer should be able to enjoy the mod as a strange little profession in the Persean Sector without ever being told that this interpretation is naive.

The other reading belongs to the player who notices.

The numbers do not add up.

The trade has no logistics.

The same boat is everywhere.

The same man is on every boat.

A crewman passes behind the Fisherman carrying a crate and reaches the other side empty-handed. Nobody asks where the crate went.

A specimen has one structure when it is unloaded and another when it reaches the scale.

A coat contains a shelf several metres away.

The Fisherman notices all of this.

Nobody else does.

The mod does not announce that this is horror.

It simply declines to correct the record.

---

# 2. The governing rule: do not explain the horror

The quickest way to ruin Catch.Release is to identify the strange thing for the player.

Do not write:

> Something about the scene is deeply wrong.

Write the scene.

Do not write:

> Crablobab's coat seems to violate the laws of space.

Have him reach farther into the coat than his arm is long and put the merchandise on the table.

Do not write:

> The Fisherman's crew have been warped by prolonged exposure to hyperspace.

Have a crewman open a locker without opening the door.

Do not write:

> The pattern is an eldritch imitation of a living animal.

Describe the gills working in the wrong order and continue with the market value.

The player is allowed to make the conclusion.

The prose is not.

### Understatement over spectacle

Catch.Release is interested in **subtraction, inconsistency, and continuity failure**, not gore.

A person with half a torso missing who continues working normally is appropriate.

A detailed description of torn organs is not.

A crew list containing a name twice is appropriate.

A screaming breakdown about doubles is not.

A specimen that continues moving after a section of it has ceased to be present is appropriate.

A paragraph about its grotesque mutilation is not.

### No winking

The narrator does not wink at the player.

Avoid:

- "somehow";
- "impossibly";
- "inexplicably";
- "against all reason";
- "you try not to think about it";
- "which is somehow worse";
- "something is very wrong";
- "you decide not to ask";
- "best not to dwell on it";
- "whatever it is";
- "eldritch";
- "cosmic";
- "unnatural";

These can occasionally be valid words in ordinary prose, but in Catch.Release they are usually a sign that the narrator is doing the player's interpretation for them.

State what happened.

Continue.

# 3. Who notices

This is the mechanism beneath almost every strange scene.

## Everyone except the Fisherman accepts reality's account

Low coherence does not usually present itself to ordinary people as a contradiction.

A thing that cannot be true simply **is true**.

Reality says the clam has three hinges. The observer sees a clam with three hinges. Reality says this crewman has always had that face. The crew roster agrees. Reality says there is a fishing industry. There are fishermen in bars, catch on menus, buyers with orders and boats working ruptures.

People do not fail to notice because they are stupid.

They fail to notice because reality is supplying the answer.

Rejecting that answer would require perceiving the inconsistency from somewhere outside the system that produced it.

Ordinary people cannot do that.

This applies to:

- the player's officers;
- bar patrons;
- buyers;
- researchers;
- independent fishermen;
- patrol crews;
- faction officials;
- Crablobab's customers;
- the Fisherman's crew;
- neutral narration.

The player may notice because the player sees events across scenes and systems.

The fiction never confirms that they are correct.

## The Fisherman is the exception

The Fisherman **does notice**.

This is not resistance to low coherence.

It is not training.

It is not a special instrument.

He can perceive the contradiction because he is no longer entirely part of the reality imposing it.

He knows when his crew have changed.

He knows when the same ship is somewhere else.

He knows when a person has been edited.

He knows that the trade does not add up.

He knows what a rupture is doing.

He knows considerably more about patterns than he says.

He also knows that forcing reality to account for its contradictions is dangerous.

So he does not.

More on this under **The Fisherman**.

# 4. The trade

People believe there are fishermen.

Plural.

It is an industry, a profession and a recognized line of work.

There are independent fishing crews. There are suppliers. There are buyers. There are standing orders. A cook may be looking for three specific catches. A private collector may want one specimen above a particular mass. A researcher may pay for a low-coherence sample.

All of these people are sincere.

The larger structure they believe they participate in does not exist.

There is one Fisherman.

He is in every inhabited system at once and nowhere in particular.

There are no freight lines moving enough catch inward to supply the markets.

There is no industrial fleet sufficient to support the trade.

No shipyard has a meaningful fishing-hull industry.

No port authority has a catch quota that explains the amount sold.

One boat could not supply a district, much less the Sector.

Fish are on menus anyway.

Nobody has done the arithmetic.

This is the same phenomenon as a third hinge on a clam, expanded to the scale of an economy.

### Independent fishermen

Independent fishers are real.

They own or work a rupture. Pirates may squeeze them for protection. Mercenaries may deny them access. The Path may sabotage their equipment.

They sincerely believe they are members of a larger profession.

They are not pretending.

Do not make an independent fisher secretly suspicious of the industry.

Do not have a clever merchant discover the missing logistics.

Nobody gets to become the second Fisherman.

# 5. The fabric

Reality has a **fabric**.

Everything in realspace exists as part of it.

The fabric can be:

- thick;
- thin;
- worn;
- strained;
- holding;
- going;
- torn.

Hyperspace is on the other side.

Do not commit to literal geometry.

"Through" is useful.

"Under" is sometimes useful.

"Beyond" is occasionally useful.

There is no canonical human direction corresponding to the relationship.

A breach is not literally a hole in a sheet suspended in space. The cloth language is a useful description of something human spatial language does not represent well.

## There is no hyperspace ocean

The fabric is not water.

Nothing in hyperspace is literally:

- submerged;
- swimming through an ocean;
- floating beneath a surface;
- wet;
- underwater.

Do not casually turn the setting into nautical fantasy.

The fishing language is occupational slang layered over something that is not actually a sea.

### "The water"

Ordinary people may occasionally say **the water**.

A dockhand, cook, buyer, independent fisher, rating or other lay speaker may use it because fishing has supplied their vocabulary.

They are not making a philosophical claim.

They have simply never needed another word.

Use this rarely.

Never use **the water** as technical terminology.

Never use it in neutral narration to describe hyperspace.

Never use it in intel written from an instrumental or survey perspective.

Never use it from the Fisherman when he is being precise.

He says **fabric**.

Real water remains water.

A tank is full of water.

A conservatory has water.

A cook may put a fish in water.

That is ordinary water and has nothing to do with the metaphor.

# 6. Coherence

**Coherence is how well reality is holding in a place.**

It is also used to describe how well something pulled through the fabric continues to hold a stable form.

Coherence is not morality.

It is not corruption.

It is not taint.

It is not an evil energy.

It is a condition.

Use **coherence**, not *cohesion*, for the setting term.

## Permanence holds reality together

Anything physical and settled contributes permanence.

Examples:

- a planet;
- a station;
- a hull;
- a person;
- a city;
- a long-established object.

Population does this particularly well.

A very large number of people being definitely present in one place is a great deal of permanence.

The Fisherman may describe it simply:

> "Fabric's thick where people are. All that living holds it down."

This is not meant as a complete physical theory.

It is the practical version.

## What wears it down

Things that strongly interact with hyperspace disturb or thin the fabric.

Examples include familiar Sector phenomena and machinery associated with extreme hyperspatial effects.

The mod does not need to enumerate a universal physics textbook.

What matters for Catch.Release is that some places are better held than others.

The abyss is not necessarily a machine or source. It is simply where very little remains to hold.

## What low coherence does to permanent things

Low coherence does not "damage" a human in the ordinary sense.

It **edits**.

Something may be added.

Something may be absent.

The person's continuity updates around the change.

There need not be:

- pain;
- blood;
- shock;
- a wound;
- a memory of an accident.

If a hand is gone, the person may simply have always worked one-handed.

If another arm is present, their uniform may already be made for it.

If their face changes, the identification image may agree.

The lower the coherence, the less reality is obliged to preserve a previous answer.

Do not overuse bodily alteration.

It loses its force quickly.

A changed name on a manifest may do more work than another missing limb.

## What low coherence does to patterns

Patterns have very little permanence to begin with.

They are particularly poor at maintaining one answer.

A low-coherence specimen may:

- change markings;
- lose or gain structure;
- drift without an obvious force;
- retain an inconsistent mass;
- produce sounds;
- present features that do not remain fixed;
- appear different in separate records;
- continue behaving after parts of its apparent body cease to hold.

It does not "die" when its shape fails.

It stops maintaining that shape.

### Specimen coherence

A specimen's coherence primarily reflects **where it came through**.

It is not a moral or genetic property of the fish.

Two catches from the same local fabric should broadly reflect the same environmental coherence.

Transport equipment can help preserve the state in which a specimen was recovered.

It cannot make the original crossing better than it was.

# 7. Breaches and ruptures

A **breach** or **rupture** is a place where the fabric is worn sufficiently thin that hyperspace is exposed through it.

Both terms are valid.

They are faults, not passages.

A rupture is not:

- a jump point;
- a stable portal;
- a gate;
- a navigable tunnel.

Passing a ship or living person through one is not what the equipment does.

The entire fishing rig exists because normal living crews cannot safely make the crossing.

### Pond

**Pond** is fishing slang.

It may appear in Fisherman or trade dialogue when the speaker is intentionally talking like a fisher.

It may appear in deliberately informal player-facing fishing UI if that register is desired.

Technical descriptions, survey prose and neutral narration should normally use **rupture** or **breach**.

Somewhere with no existing rupture is **open space**.

---

# 8. Patterns

The careful term is **pattern**.

The ordinary term is **fish**.

A pattern is a hyperspace ghost taking a temporary realspace form during retrieval.

For reasons nobody has explained, that form is very often a fish.

Sometimes it resembles:

- a crab;
- a mollusc;
- something that does not fit an ordinary category.

## A pattern is not an animal

Do not write a pattern as though it possesses an ordinary evolved biology.

It has no established:

- ecology;
- reproductive cycle;
- evolutionary history;
- natural habitat;
- biological ancestry.

Descriptions can imitate the language used for animals because that is what people do when presented with an animal-shaped object.

The underlying thing is not obligated to make anatomical sense.

A good fish description often reads like reality assembled an animal from incomplete reference material.

This does **not** mean every fish needs to be grotesque.

Most should look plausible enough at first glance.

The strange detail should earn its place.

## It does not die

A pattern can cease to hold together.

That is not necessarily death.

Avoid narration such as:

> The creature dies.

Prefer the state of the object:

> The pattern loses its shape.

or simply describe what remains.

## Looking

A pattern that looks at a person is not necessarily observing them as an animal would.

The Fisherman knows this distinction matters.

He does not fully explain it.

His language may be as simple as:

> "They look."

or:

> "Don't mistake that for seeing you."

Do not follow that with a paragraph explaining exactly what consciousness a pattern possesses.

## Rarity

Rarity means rarity.

It does not inherently mean:

- larger;
- older;
- more powerful;
- more dangerous;
- deeper;
- more intelligent.

A rare fish is one encountered less often.

Any other property belongs to that pattern specifically.

## Buried patterns

Some patterns are harder to reach because they lie further into the fabric.

Fishing language may describe them as buried, deep or hiding.

This does not imply a literal water column.

Breach-light equipment can expose or betray patterns that ordinary retrieval cannot easily reach.

---

# 9. Eating patterns

Mostly safe.

This is important.

Catch.Release is not a setting where everyone is unknowingly eating lethal cosmic parasites.

People eat the catch because much of it is actually edible.

That is the commercial basis of the trade.

Individual species may have specific culinary or physiological effects.

Those belong in the appropriate species description.

## Low-coherence catch

Eating a badly coherent specimen is different.

The consumer takes some of that instability with them.

Their own coherence may begin to drop.

The trade does not have a proper in-universe understanding of this.

Do not have ordinary cooks or researchers correctly explain the mechanism.

The market does not automatically price low-coherence catch as hazardous biological material.

---

# 10. The ROD

The ROD is the central technology of the trade.

**It allows equipment to pass through a breach into hyperspace and return.**

Everything else is a way to use or deliver that capability.

The D stands for **Device**.

The R and O are unresolved.

Everyone nevertheless says **ROD device**.

This is universal and slightly stupid.

Leave it that way.

## The ROD is contextual

Away from a rupture or suitably weakened fabric, a ROD is mostly just hardware.

It requires the appropriate interaction with the fabric to do what makes it unusual.

This matters when a ROD-equipped harpoon hits a ship.

The hull suffers the consequences of a very large projectile.

The projectile does not automatically open the ship into hyperspace.

## Living passage

ROD equipment is not a safe human transport technology.

Do not write it as a secret alternative jump drive.

Living material exposed to the process does not return in a condition that supports calling the experiment successful.

The Fisherman knows more about this than he says.

## Legality

The ROD is not respectable civilian equipment.

The Church and Path have obvious religious objections.

Other authorities view it as unlicensed, hazardous technology at best.

Patrol dialogue should focus on things a patrol can actually observe and regulate:

- unregistered hardware;
- hazardous operation;
- heavy projectiles;
- interference;
- damage;
- unauthorized high-energy devices.

Ordinary patrol officers should not accurately lecture the player about the metaphysics of the fabric.

---

# 11. LYNE drones and the LINE

LYNE drones are the primary ROD retrieval platforms.

The acronym currently has no expansion.

Do not invent one casually.

If an expansion is eventually required, it should sound like a dull technical backronym devised by engineers or procurement staff, not a dramatic lore phrase.

## Scale

The drones are **fighter-sized**.

They are not hand-carried probes.

The ROD/LINE installation is substantial shipboard machinery.

Moving it involves:

- cargo cranes;
- handling frames;
- heavy deck crews;
- launch racks;
- external clamps;
- service assemblies.

When the Fisherman gives the player their first rig, it should feel closer to receiving auxiliary craft than receiving a suitcase.

## LINE

"LINE" refers to the drone-side retrieval system / fishing arrangement.

The visible retrieval line is not an ordinary mechanical fishing line.

Do not narrate:

- a winch winding it in;
- a steel cable going taut;
- a reel pulling the fish aboard;
- knots tied into the line.

The visual exists because the player needs to understand the interaction.

Fishing dialogue can still call it:

- the line;
- the rope.

That is slang.

The mechanism is not literally rope.

---

# 12. The harpoon

The harpoon is an aimed, high-speed ROD delivery and retrieval system.

It is the aggressive alternative to sending drones patiently through an existing rupture.

It is also a very large projectile.

This explains why putting one through a ship generates substantial legal interest without requiring it to perform exotic effects against the hull.

The Fisherman's practical summary can be blunt:

> "Heavy projectile. Treat it accordingly."

If challenged that it is obviously a weapon, he may answer from occupational perspective rather than deny the physics.

Do not make him pretend a multi-tonne launcher is harmless.

## Retrieval trace

The visible line associated with the harpoon is gameplay communication.

It is not a steel cable.

Dialogue may say *line* or *rope*.

Technical narration should not turn it into a winch system.

---

# 13. Breach lights

Breach lights are badly named.

They do not meaningfully illuminate the target.

They **weaken the fabric where they are directed**, allowing ROD interaction where no natural rupture is currently open.

They make a breach to order.

This is why using them near inhabited space is treated so seriously.

## Near people

Low coherence near a crewed station or fleet can edit people.

The effect does not need malice.

The light does not know there are people there.

Reality simply has less ability to insist on the previous answer.

This can affect whole crews.

## Faction knowledge

Patrols do not need to understand this correctly.

They know:

- the device produces alarming readings;
- its operation is prohibited;
- it is dangerous near inhabited traffic;
- repeat offenders deserve progressively stronger intervention.

The Church and Path may interpret it through doctrine.

That does not mean they have Fisherman-level technical understanding.

---

# 14. Bycatch

Hyperspace contains wreckage and lost material.

A retrieval rig can bring some of it back.

Derelict fragments, cargo and similar finds need little explanation.

More improbable loot is a gameplay concession.

Do not invent an elaborate theory to justify every blueprint or rare item recovered through fishing.

Leaving something unexplained is often cleaner than explaining it badly.

---

# 15. Range data

Range data describes where a particular pattern tends to occur.

It can identify:

- range;
- likely systems;
- relevant local conditions.

The data is correct.

The mechanism by which the trade collectively knows enough to produce these charts does not withstand much scrutiny.

Nobody has performed that scrutiny.

Do not solve this.

The Fisherman may provide range data.

The trade may buy and sell it.

The map may use it.

It works.

## Information-release order

The tutorial should introduce range data only after the player understands basic ROD fishing and the Harpoon/Breach Light loop.

The first tutorial range-data handoff gives **two common targets**.

This is also the point where the fishing planner is explained, because the player now has more than one destination and therefore a reason to plan a route.

After completing that two-target assignment, range-data purchasing unlocks and the player receives:

- two common range entries;
- one uncommon range entry;
- one rare range entry.

These rarity assignments are progression design, not an in-world statement that rare fish are stronger.

# 16. The Fisherman

The Fisherman was once a human mercenary captain.

After the Gate Collapse, he worked with a scientist on the technology that became the ROD. Like many people in that period, they were trying methods that might restore forms of movement the Sector had lost.

He spent too long fishing.

Too long beside ruptures.

Too long in low-coherence fabric.

He is not human any more.

That sentence should not be understood as "he has mutated badly."

He became something categorically different.

## What he is now

The Fisherman is a **conscious expression of the rupture's wrongness**.

He is what prolonged low-coherence exposure made from the original captain.

The memories, habits, professional competence and personality of that man remain.

So does the occupation.

But he no longer sits comfortably inside the reality ordinary people inhabit.

He is partly beyond the mechanism that makes contradictions self-consistent.

This is why he can perceive them.

Do not reduce this to:

- advanced hyperspace senses;
- psychic ability;
- immunity;
- a special mutation;
- an AI;
- possession;
- a secret alien;
- an avatar of a named god.

There is no need to place another entity behind him.

The rupture is enough.

## He knows

The Fisherman is the only character in the mod who understands that the impossible things are impossible.

He sees his crew change.

He sees the same boat elsewhere.

He sees when reality alters a person and supplies them with a matching history.

He sees the trade's false continuity.

He understands the relationship between coherence, fabric and patterns at a level nobody else reaches.

He knows that reality is actively smoothing contradictions around ordinary observers.

He also knows what happens when you force reality to confront one of those contradictions too precisely.

It gets upset.

That phrase is intentionally colloquial.

Do not turn it into a named physical law unless the mod later needs one.

## Why he does not explain

The Fisherman is not coy.

He is not protecting the player's fragile mind.

He does not speak in riddles to seem mysterious.

He is not trying to preserve a supernatural secret.

He has a practical reason.

**Explanation forces definition. Definition makes reality account for the thing being defined.**

Sometimes that is a poor idea.

So he stops.

He redirects.

He gives the useful half of the answer.

He uses fishing language where exact language would pull too hard on the problem.

His restraint is occupational safety.

Example:

> "You left a boat there."
>
> "This one's here."

If pressed:

> "Some knots hold better if you don't pull every loose end."
>
> "Get your fish."

He knows this does not answer the question.

That is why he said it.

## He does not care that he is wrong

The Fisherman is not fighting his condition.

He is not searching for a cure.

He is not mourning his humanity in every conversation.

He has had time to incorporate ontological impossibility into the working day.

He is still capable of:

- amusement;
- irritation;
- generosity;
- professional pride;
- dry humor;
- interest in a good catch;
- giving practical advice.

He simply does not regard being what he is as a crisis.

## Voice

The Fisherman should have a **real voice**.

The previous instruction to write him "flat" is superseded.

Write him as an old, heavily experienced spacer-fisher who has seen every ordinary disaster and several categories of disaster reality does not officially contain.

He is:

- grizzled;
- practical;
- technically competent;
- dry;
- patient when patience is useful;
- capable of small jokes;
- unhurried;
- comfortable in command;
- helpful;
- difficult to surprise.

He is **not**:

- a pirate caricature;
- a folksy oracle;
- a cowboy;
- a wizard;
- a cryptic anime mentor;
- an emotionless exposition device;
- constantly ominous.

Do not fill every line with:

- "aye";
- "lad";
- nautical idioms;
- metaphors about storms;
- cryptic warnings.

One occupational phrase in the right place is stronger than six.

He was a mercenary captain and a technical collaborator before he became this.

The education and competence should remain visible beneath the fisher's habits.

### Useful Fisherman cadence

He often:

1. gives the practical answer;
2. notices the deeper question;
3. decides whether answering it is worth the risk;
4. stops.

Example:

> "Pattern."
>
> He turns the container slightly.
>
> "Fish is fine if you're buying supper. Pattern's the careful word."

Or:

> "Not water."
>
> "Thinner fabric. Pattern gets less help holding one shape."
>
> He looks at you.
>
> "Same rule applies to the crew. Keep the trip short."

The line does not need an ominous tag afterward.

## Helpful means helpful

There is no secret catch to dealing with him.

He provides equipment.

He teaches the player.

He offers work.

He sells range data.

He may give good advice.

Do not add betrayal merely because he is strange.

The fact that a fundamentally wrong thing can be sincerely helpful is more interesting.

## Baha

His old registry name is **Baha**.

He does not normally use it.

Paperwork still identifies him that way.

A fitting response is mundane rather than portentous:

> "Registry says Baha."
>
> "Fisherman will do."

## Name degradation

Where the fabric is sufficiently thin, his displayed name may degrade.

The degradation is a property of place/coherence, not random dramatic static.

If it drops specific letters at a given location, that should be consistent there.

Do not use name corruption every time he appears.

## What he does with the catch

Never explain it.

Do not hint at a secret answer.

Do not write a coy line implying he eats them, feeds something, builds something, sacrifices them or uses them to maintain himself.

There is no answer available to the fiction.

---

# 17. The Fisherman's crew

The crew are also heavily altered.

Unlike the Fisherman, they do **not** possess his privileged awareness.

They remain within reality's self-correction.

They may:

- trade places;
- trade faces;
- reach through solid things;
- appear in the wrong part of a compartment;
- change between camera cuts;
- possess inconsistent names;
- perform actions incompatible with their visible bodies.

They do not object.

They do not discuss it.

Reality tells them this is ordinary.

The Fisherman sees it.

He usually does not care.

### Writing crew anomalies

Keep them incidental.

Bad:

> One of the Fisherman's horribly mutated crewmen reaches impossibly through a locker door.

Better:

> A crewman reaches through the closed locker and removes a wrench.

Then continue the conversation.

The anomaly should often occupy a subordinate clause or one clean sentence.

Do not stop the scene to admire it.

## The scientist

The scientist who worked with the Fisherman on the ROD is aboard.

He merged into the crew.

He is there.

He is not cleanly one person anymore.

Do not identify a specific crewman as secretly being "the scientist".

The boundary is no longer useful enough to support that answer.

---

# 18. The fleet

The Fisherman's fleet is a ghost fleet in the broad Flying Dutchman sense.

It appears where it should not.

It moves without needing to travel between appearances.

Mechanically, several instances may exist in inhabited systems and on the frontier.

Fictionally, this is not:

- a franchise;
- cloned captains;
- identical siblings;
- a distributed organization using the same name;
- a hologram system.

It is the Fisherman.

He is in more than one place.

That is the answer.

Do not provide another one.

## The player may ask

The player is allowed to notice.

A useful exchange:

> "I just left you in another system."
>
> "You left a boat there."
>
> "This one's here."

The Fisherman is not pretending to misunderstand.

He simply knows how far to continue.

---

# 19. Crablobab

Crablobab is a low-coherence being associated with the Fisherman's fleet.

He has the rough shape and social habits of a man.

His coat is less successful at the arrangement.

He is cheerful.

He is funny.

He likes commerce.

He is also about as thoroughly anomalous as a person can become and still operate a stall.

## Do not make him sinister

Crablobab does not lurk.

He does not threaten.

He does not lower his voice to announce forbidden knowledge.

He is delighted to have customers.

His scenes should be funny before they are frightening.

The frightening part comes later, if the player reviews what actually occurred.

## Do not make him random

"Mad" does not mean word salad.

Crablobab understands:

- prices;
- products;
- customers;
- trades;
- crabs;
- whether you have paid him.

He can complete a transaction.

His associations may be peculiar, but the scene should remain readable.

The joke is that his business functions perfectly while the physical facts do not.

## Show the anomaly

Good Crablobab details:

- an item larger than the pocket it comes from;
- the inside of the coat containing shelves;
- a hand already occupying the pocket into which he puts something;
- stock registered before the sale occurs;
- a backdrop being rolled up with less wall remaining afterward;
- something inside the coat knocking back;
- crabs appearing where they were not packed.

Do not write:

> Crablobab bends reality.

Show the receipt.

## Nobody comments on the coat

This includes narration.

Bad:

> You decide not to ask how the coat contains so much space.

Better:

> Crablobab reaches past a shelf several metres inside the coat and retrieves the box.

Then:

> "Celebration charges!"

## The earmuffs

The earmuffs work.

Put them on and the wearer stops hearing the whispering associated with thin fabric.

The whispering itself does not stop.

A recorder may still contain it.

Another person may still hear it.

Crablobab does not explain the distinction.

He is not tricking anyone.

The customer asked for the whispering to stop bothering them.

As far as he is concerned, the product meets specification.

# 20. Why people fish

Patterns began appearing beneath ruptures.

Somebody found a way to retrieve them.

Many are edible.

That is enough.

Do not overcomplicate the commercial origin.

Catch can be:

- food;
- curios;
- research material;
- private collection pieces;
- entertainment;
- gambling stock;
- prestige purchases.

The buyers usually do not understand where the thing ultimately came from.

They do not need to.

---

# 21. Breach Conservatories

A colony may construct a **Breach Conservatory**.

It is both:

- public display space;
- local fishing outfitter/trade infrastructure.

Its tanks contain catch.

The stock does not die in the ordinary way.

Nobody treats that as a philosophical emergency.

It is a civic attraction.

Families visit.

Children look through the glass.

Do not make the building itself a haunted-house attraction unless a specific piece of content calls for it.

Its wrongness should remain embedded in normal municipal life.

# 22. Tutorial information order

The tutorial is also the player's lore introduction.

Do not give information earlier simply because a dialogue box has room.

## Entry

The player may be directed to the Fisherman by a rating or other ordinary source.

If the player asks what a **rating** is, explain it in-world: non-officer ship's crew, working deck/drives/weapons/flight/loading roles depending on berth.

Alternatively, a player who discovers unstable fabric before finding the Fisherman may recover one of his possessions.

This object should be something like a damaged LYNE service assembly, docking frame or transponder package.

It is:

- heavy;
- crane-handled;
- clearly associated with the Fisherman;
- not usable as premature Harpoon equipment.

Its purpose is to point the player toward the fleet.

## First Fisherman encounter

Explain only what the player needs to use the ROD/LINE installation.

Show its scale.

Give the equipment.

Send the player to a nearby natural rupture.

Do not explain the entire cosmology.

## First catch return

Now explain:

- LYNE drones;
- basic ROD retrieval;
- fish versus pattern;
- "ghost" as old spacer/trade language.

The Fisherman does not explain why patterns are fish.

## Second assignment

Send the player to another near-Core system with visibly lower coherence.

The Fisherman is there too.

The player may challenge this.

He does not resolve it.

## Second catch return

Introduce the outfitter.

Then grant:

- Breach Lights;
- Harpoon.

Explain their practical operation.

Do not show equipment/tackle shop entries for hardware the player does not own.

Do not reveal Harpoon or Breach Light categories prematurely.

## Third catch

Require the player to use the Breach Light + Harpoon loop in open space.

A special/golden tutorial catch is appropriate.

## Third return

Unlock the remaining owned-equipment shop categories.

Give two common range entries.

Explain range data.

Explain the fishing planner because the player now has two simultaneous destinations.

## Final assignment

Retrieve both charted patterns.

On return:

- unlock range-data purchasing;
- award two common range entries;
- one uncommon range entry;
- one rare range entry.

At this point the player understands enough to explore the system independently.

# 23. Who knows what

This boundary matters enormously.

## Ordinary buyers

May know:

- market terminology;
- quality;
- weight;
- appearance;
- handling;
- rarity;
- price.

Do not make them ontologists.

## Cooks

Treat catch as ingredients.

They may be particular.

They are not secretly studying hyperspace.

## Independent fishermen

Know practical gear and fishing procedure.

They sincerely believe in the industry.

They do not know the truth about the Fisherman's existence.

## Researchers and the Academy

May know measurable facts:

- coherence values;
- deformation;
- mass discrepancy;
- morphology;
- retrieval telemetry;
- structural instability.

They can say:

> "The mass return is inconsistent."

They should not say:

> "Reality is rewriting the specimen."

That conclusion belongs to the Fisherman.

## Patrols

Know:

- laws;
- damage;
- registration;
- sensor readings;
- prohibited equipment;
- dangerous behavior.

They do not need to understand fabric theory in order to fine or shoot the player.

## The Church

May frame the technology as trespass, arrogance or sinful interference.

That is doctrine, not proof that they know the actual mechanism.

## The Path

May hate the equipment more violently.

They still do not become correct cosmic physicists because they are angry about technology.

## Crablobab

His degree of conscious understanding is not a question the mod needs to answer.

He demonstrates.

He does not lecture.

## The Fisherman

Knows.

Stops talking before knowledge becomes definition.

# 24. Register: what things are called

| Thing | Careful / technical | Slang / ordinary speech | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reality's substrate | fabric | water, very rarely and only from lay speakers | veil, aether |
| Worn-through fabric | breach, rupture | pond, spot | portal, jump point, gate |
| Hyperspace ghost | pattern | fish | creature, animal |
| Retrieval craft | LYNE drones | drones, line | robots, little probes |
| Retrieval connection | — | line, rope | cable, winch line |
| Fabric-weakeners | breach lights | lights, lamps, searchlights | floodlights |
| Local reality condition | coherence | thin, worn, holding, going | corruption, taint |
| No natural breach | open space | — | open water |
| Pattern distribution | range | where they turn up | waters, biome |

"Species" may still be used as a **game-data/table classification** because the player needs a usable category.

Avoid making the fiction claim that patterns belong to conventional biological species in a scientific sense.

# 25. Starsector prose: the baseline

Catch.Release should read as though it belongs inside Starsector before it reads as though it belongs inside a horror story.

This section is specifically for writers and language models such as SOL.

## People have jobs

Characters should sound like people who already had a working day before the player opened the comm channel.

An ops chief thinks about:

- manifests;
- mass;
- deck space;
- cranes;
- repairs;
- crew assignments;
- invoices.

A science officer thinks about:

- telemetry;
- sensor returns;
- measurements;
- inconsistencies;
- calibration.

A patrol officer thinks about:

- jurisdiction;
- incident categories;
- orders;
- liability;
- whether this becomes somebody else's problem.

A cook thinks about the plate.

A trader thinks about price.

Do not turn every NPC into a vessel for theme.

Theme works better when the cook remains worried about dinner.

## Competence is normal

The Sector is harsh and most people who survive in space know their jobs.

Do not make supporting characters helpless so the protagonist can look impressive.

A crew encountering strange equipment usually starts by:

- checking the manifest;
- reading the telemetry;
- identifying the mass;
- asking what can be disconnected;
- determining who has jurisdiction.

That competence gives the strange details something solid to push against.

## Narration observes before it interprets

Prefer:

> Your ops chief checks the mass figure twice.

to:

> Your ops chief is visibly disturbed by the impossible mass.

Prefer:

> The registry entry already contains the transfer.

to:

> Somehow, impossibly, the registry knows what happened.

Prefer:

> The link opens on an empty chair. Someone off-screen is reading your transponder code.

to:

> The abandoned channel creates an ominous sense that the other crew is preparing something terrible.

Observation lets the player do the emotional work.

## Concrete nouns beat decorative adjectives

Use:

- slate;
- bulkhead;
- cargo boom;
- docking frame;
- transponder return;
- tank lid;
- flight deck;
- control unit;
- maintenance tag;
- sensor plot.

Do not rely on:

- eerie;
- uncanny;
- ominous;
- sinister;
- eldritch;
- unsettling;
- bizarre.

The object is more convincing than the adjective.

## Use technical language selectively

Starsector prose is comfortable around technical and bureaucratic language.

That does not mean every sentence needs invented jargon.

A specific technical noun makes the world feel real.

Five unexplained technical nouns in one sentence make the writer feel insecure.

Use the term that matters.

Move on.

# 26. Sentence rhythm

Do not write every paragraph at the same length.

Do not write every sentence with the same clause pattern.

A useful rhythm is:

- a medium observational sentence;
- another sentence that adds the practical detail;
- a short line;
- dialogue;
- perhaps one final observation.

Example:

> The first LYNE drone crosses on external clamps. It is roughly the size of a light fighter, with the ROD assembly buried inside a blunt maneuvering frame.
>
> A second follows.
>
> "That's the line," the Fisherman says.
>
> Your ops crew are already arguing about where to bolt down the launch rack.

The short sentence is not dramatic merely because it is short.

It gives the prose room.

## Do not overuse commas

If a sentence has accumulated three independent descriptive clauses, consider stopping it.

Bad:

> The Fisherman turns, looking toward the window, his expression unreadable, while somewhere behind him a crewman moves past, carrying a crate that seems too heavy for him.

Better:

> The Fisherman looks toward the window.
>
> A crewman passes behind him with a cargo crate.

If the crate needs to become strange, do it in the next sentence.

## Dashes

Use them when the interruption actually matters.

Do not make every aside a dash.

Starsector prose often benefits from clean stops.

# 27. Dialogue

Dialogue should sound spoken.

That does not mean writing every hesitation and filler word.

Characters usually know what they want from the player.

Give them the shortest natural route to it.

## Avoid exposition speeches

If the Fisherman needs to teach three things, make three beats.

Player:

> "What did I catch?"

Fisherman:

> "Pattern."
>
> He turns the container.
>
> "Fish is fine if you're buying supper. Pattern's the careful word."

Then let the player ask the next question.

Do not have him deliver a five-paragraph lecture because all the information is technically relevant.

## Subtext

Characters do not have to explain what their behavior means.

A corporate officer with the invoice already prepared does not need narration saying Tri-Tachyon is ruthlessly commercial.

A Hegemony officer passing the recovered ROD head to Naval Intelligence does not need a paragraph about Hegemony suspicion.

The act is the characterization.

## Repetition

Natural repetition is useful when it belongs to the speaker.

Mechanical repetition is not.

Crablobab can enthusiastically repeat a product name.

A frightened bureaucrat may repeat a regulation number.

Do not have every character repeat the player's question before answering.

---

# 28. Player dialogue options

Options should express **intent**, not paraphrase the paragraph the player just read.

Good:

- "What exactly is a rating?"
- "You give this equipment to every stranger?"
- "I just left you in another system."
- "This is a weapon."
- "Where does the range data come from?"
- "No fish fighting."
- Decline.
- Pay the fine.
- Cut the comm link.

Avoid:

- overly literary player lines;
- jokes that force the player's captain to have one personality;
- options consisting of a full paragraph;
- multiple choices that differ only cosmetically unless roleplay is the point.

When several human responses are plausible, provide them.

When there is no meaningful decision, use **Continue**.

Do not force the player to say something theatrical simply to advance text.

---

# 29. Humor

Catch.Release should be funny.

The humor is usually dry and situational.

Good sources of humor:

- bureaucracy encountering fishing hardware;
- a serious officer having to write "harpoon" on a naval incident form;
- children treating a fish tournament as binding diplomacy;
- an entrepreneur discovering that one successful delivery constitutes a supply chain;
- Crablobab operating a perfectly functional shop from impossible pockets;
- the Fisherman treating existential abnormalities as equipment-maintenance concerns.

The joke should generally be played straight.

Do not put a punchline tag after every funny event.

Example:

> The blueprint is entered under COLONY INFRASTRUCTURE.
>
> The category was already present.

Stop.

---

# 30. Horror

Horror is not a separate voice.

Do not switch from "Starsector prose" to "horror prose" when the strange thing appears.

That switch tells the player when to be scared.

Instead, keep the same voice.

A normal manifest and an impossible manifest should be described with the same grammar.

This is essential.

The best Catch.Release horror often occurs several seconds after the player has read the line.

---

# 31. Faction voice

Faction dialogue should be recognizable through priorities and institutional habits, not caricatured accents.

## Hegemony

Think:

- Navy;
- registry;
- lawful authority;
- procedure;
- chain of command;
- Naval Intelligence;
- controlled irritation when reality does not fit the form.

They are not universally stupid or fascist parody officers.

A Hegemony officer should sound like someone whose institution expects the Sector to be legible.

## Tri-Tachyon

Think:

- liability;
- accounts;
- corporate security;
- ownership;
- contract;
- escalation;
- litigation;
- cost.

Keep the menace professional.

The funniest Tri-Tachyon line is often an invoice.

## Luddic Church

Think:

- moral seriousness;
- stewardship;
- restraint;
- concern for ordinary people;
- suspicion of reckless technology.

Do not write every Church character as a fanatic.

Faith can be completely sincere and completely ordinary.

## Luddic Path

Think:

- ideological certainty;
- direct language;
- hostility toward transgressive technology;
- little interest in administrative compromise.

Do not make every Pather rant.

A calm Pather can be more convincing.

## Persean League

Think:

- law;
- member-state jurisdiction;
- committees;
- negotiated authority;
- overlapping statutes.

Bureaucratic complexity can be comic without making League officers buffoons.

## Sindrian Diktat

Think:

- formal authority;
- compliance;
- administrative threat;
- certainty that the state gets the final word.

Avoid cartoon fascist speeches.

## Independents

Think:

- practical;
- contractual;
- tired;
- local;
- concerned with fuel, repairs and whether this job is worth the trouble.

They often have the least ideological language.

## Pirates

Think:

- opportunistic;
- practical;
- irreverent;
- willing to appreciate something funny until it becomes expensive.

Do not make every pirate a theatrical rogue.

# 32. Descriptions and UI text

## Tooltips

A tooltip should first tell the player what the thing does.

Flavor comes second.

Do not bury mechanics inside atmosphere.

## Intel

Intel is normally more precise than casual dialogue.

Use:

- rupture;
- range;
- coherence;
- survey;
- open space.

Avoid casual water language unless the intel is explicitly quoting someone.

## Fish descriptions

Describe what is actually observable.

Possible structure:

1. familiar visual comparison;
2. one or two precise deviations;
3. practical note, culinary note or handling consequence;
4. stop.

Do not explain the ghost underneath every fish.

The player already knows what system they are using.

## Colony descriptions

Describe the facility as a functioning place.

People visit it.

Workers maintain it.

There are tanks, fittings, walkways, ticket booths, service access.

If something impossible happens, do not transform the whole paragraph into Gothic prose.

# 33. Common failure modes

## Purple prose

Bad:

> The impossible ichor of another reality shimmers beneath the tortured veil of existence.

Wrong setting, wrong vocabulary, wrong volume.

## Anime characterization

Avoid:

- exaggerated flustered reactions;
- theatrical gasps;
- characters freezing in shock over minor oddities;
- smug mysterious smiles after every cryptic line;
- repeated "..." as personality;
- people loudly announcing their emotional states.

Humans can be surprised.

They should react like working adults.

## Constant emotional interpretation

Avoid:

> He says with a joyless smile.

unless the smile matters.

Avoid:

> She sounds almost pleased.

unless the ambiguity matters.

Avoid:

> with the particular patience of someone cataloguing a sin.

as a recurring construction.

Occasional interpretive comparisons can be excellent.

Repeated use becomes authorial mannerism.

## Repeated "someone who..." similes

The existing draft overuses forms like:

> with the expression of someone who...
>
> in the voice of somebody who...
>
> the way someone does when...

Use these rarely.

Describe the behavior itself.

## Fake ominousness

Avoid:

> For a moment, nobody speaks.
>
> Then the Fisherman smiles.

unless the silence and smile have concrete significance.

Do not manufacture suspense around ordinary information.

## Overexplaining the joke

If a League officer discovers there is no form for being hit by a fishing harpoon, that is enough.

Do not add narration explaining that League bureaucracy has finally met something it cannot categorize.

# 34. A practical SOL writing procedure

When generating new Catch.Release prose, use this order.

### 1. Identify the speaker's job

What do they actually do?

What vocabulary would their work give them?

### 2. Identify what they can know

Are they the Fisherman?

If not, remove any inference that requires seeing through reality's self-consistency.

### 3. Identify the useful information

What does the player actually need from this exchange?

Lead with that.

### 4. Choose one concrete detail

Not three.

One slate.

One damaged glove.

One incorrect timestamp.

One crewman passing through a locker.

One impossible pocket.

### 5. Do not interpret the detail

Let it remain.

### 6. Read for cadence

If three consecutive paragraphs have the same structure, rewrite one.

If every sentence contains two commas, cut.

If every exchange ends with a clever final line, remove some.

### 7. Remove horror adjectives

Search for:

- eerie;
- eldritch;
- uncanny;
- impossible;
- ominous;
- disturbing;
- unsettling;
- horrifying;
- bizarre.

Ask whether the noun/action can do the work instead.

### 8. Remove explanation

If the final sentence explains why the preceding detail is strange, delete the final sentence and reread.

It is often better.

# 35. Quick calibration examples

## Ordinary Starsector observation

Good:

> The transfer manifest arrives before the cargo boom has finished extending. Your ops chief checks the mass, assigns a section of heavy deck and sends two crews down to clear it.

This makes the ship feel inhabited by competent people.

## Catch.Release anomaly

Good:

> The manifest lists both LYNE drones before the first one crosses.
>
> Your ops chief signs it.

No commentary required.

## Fisherman

Good:

> "Fabric's thin there."
>
> He enlarges the coherence plot.
>
> "Get in, get your fish, get out. Don't turn a short job into research."

Voice, advice and hidden knowledge coexist.

## Crablobab

Good:

> Crablobab opens the coat and reaches past a shelf several metres inside it.
>
> "Earmuffs!"
>
> He puts them on the table.

Do not add a paragraph about spatial impossibility.

## Patrol

Good:

> "Hegemony Navy. Heave to."
>
> The officer brings up the damage image.
>
> "I am looking at a fishing harpoon in a registered hull. Start with your transponder code."

The situation is funny because the officer is treating it seriously.

# 36. Settled unknowns

These are not gaps to fill.

Do not answer them.

- What the Fisherman ultimately does with the catch he takes.
- Why patterns so often become fish.
- How the Fisherman is simultaneously present on multiple boats.
- Where Crablobab gets his inventory.
- How range data about the far side can be correct.
- What produces the whispering in thin fabric.
- What LYNE stands for.
- What R and O stand for in ROD.

The Fisherman may know some or all of these.

That does not make them available to the prose.

---

# 37. Open questions

These may be defined later if the mod needs them.

- LYNE expansion.
- ROD expansion beyond D = Device.
- Additional practical details of how Fisherman multiplicity behaves at extreme coherence.
- Specific species-level effects of consumption.
- Further industrial uses for high/low-coherence patterns.

Do not answer an open question merely to make one dialogue line easier to write.

---

# 38. Out-of-universe touchstones

These are calibration references only.

Never mention them in fiction.

- **DREDGE** — a pleasant fishing loop capable of carrying something much worse underneath it.
- **The King in Yellow** — continuity and ordinary behavior persisting around things that should invalidate them.
- **Warhammer 40,000's Warp** — ordinary people already understand hyperspace as fundamentally hostile; Catch.Release does not need to convince them of that.
- **The Flying Dutchman** — the Fisherman's fleet as something that arrives rather than travels.

These are tonal references, not lore sources.

Catch.Release still has to sound like **Starsector**.

---

# 39. Canon and source discipline

Starsector mods are not canon sources for Catch.Release unless this project explicitly establishes otherwise.

When writing material that touches the broader Sector:

1. use vanilla Starsector setting/history as the authority;
2. use Catch.Release lore for this mod's additions;
3. do not import another mod's faction, terminology, interpretation or history because it sounds appropriate.

If a broad-setting detail is not required, do not invent it merely to decorate the sentence.

A generic "yard", "patrol", "station" or "trade office" is often better than an unnecessary named institution.

---

# 40. Final writing test

Before accepting a line, ask:

**Would this still sound at home in Starsector if the supernatural element were removed?**

If not, the baseline voice is probably wrong.

Then ask:

**Did the prose tell the player what to feel about the strange element?**

If yes, remove that instruction.

Then:

**Does this character know something only the Fisherman should know?**

If yes, rewrite it as observation, measurement, doctrine, rumor or ignorance.

Then:

**Did the Fisherman explain something the setting is stronger for leaving unresolved?**

If yes, stop him earlier.

Then:

**Did Crablobab explain the joke?**

If yes, let him sell the thing instead.

Finally:

**Can one sentence be removed without losing information?**

Remove it.

Catch.Release works best when it leaves the player with one detail more than the explanation can contain.
