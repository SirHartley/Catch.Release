# Catch.Release — the lore

What the mod is actually about, and the rules for writing anything new in it.

Read this before adding a line of prose to `rules.csv`, a species description to `fish.csv`, a
tackle blurb, an intel note or a tooltip. The mod's writing is load-bearing: it is the only place
the setting exists, and one row that misunderstands the premise undoes a great deal of quiet work.

---

## The one rule about tone

**On the surface this is a fun, whimsical premise. Underneath it there is nothing good.**

Both readings have to be true at once, and the surface one has to be the one that is *stated*.
Fishing into hyperspace, harpooning ghosts, a genial trader in a coat with too many pockets — a
player who never looks closely should have a lovely time and never be told they are wrong to.

The horror is never announced. It is available. It lives in what is *not* said: a number that does
not add up, a crew standing in a different order after the lights sweep past, a specimen that
"continues afterwards, on the inside". Nobody in the fiction is horrified, which is the point —
they are all far too used to it, or far too far gone to notice.

Concretely, when writing:

- **Never explain the horror.** If a line says the thing out loud it is the wrong line. The
  Fisherman's own answers are the model: *"They look at it."* — and then a pause, and then something
  worse that still does not explain anything.
- **Nobody reacts correctly.** The ops chief signs for the crate. The science officer stops taking
  notes and says nothing about why. The wrongness is ordinary to everyone present.
- **Understatement over spectacle.** "Half a torso, gone, without changes to how they work or live"
  is the register. Not gore — subtraction.
- **The player's crew are the audience surrogate**, and even they are acclimatising. That is its
  own horror and should never be pointed at.
- **No winking.** The mod never signals that it knows. It is a fishing mod.

## The fabric

Reality has a fabric. It is the thing everything sits in, and it can be thin, worn, or torn.

Hyperspace is on the other side of it. Not "above" or "below" — the fabric has no direction that
maps to a human one; a breach opens onto hyperspace the way a fourth dimension opens onto the three
you have. Prose should use *through* and *under* loosely and never commit to a geometry.

**Water is the fabric.** This is the mod's central metaphor and it is a metaphor the *characters*
use, not a description of anything. When somebody says the water is thin, bad, deep or good, they
mean the local state of reality. When somebody says a specimen came out of bad water, they mean it
was pulled through somewhere reality was barely holding, and it shows.

Hyperspace itself is about as hospitable as the worst thing you have ever read about it being.
Exposure warps mind and body. Nobody sane goes in. Nothing that lives there is a person.

## Coherence

**Coherence is how well reality is holding in a given place**, and by extension how well anything
pulled out of it is holding its shape. It is the mod's one continuous scale and everything reads off
it: what a breach looks like, what a specimen comes up like, what a place does to the people
standing in it, what the sector map paints.

Low coherence is not a hazard in the ordinary sense. It does not damage you. It *edits* you, and it
does not consider the edit to be an event. Extra limbs. A missing half. No pain, no bleeding, no
change to how you work or live — the thing is simply not there any more and never was, as far as
reality is now concerned.

Being near a breach is what exposes you. Breach lights (see below) make a breach where there was
none, which is why they are the most feared piece of equipment in this mod's world.

A specimen's coherence is a property of *where it was taken*, not of the specimen. Two things pulled
through the same breach read nearly the same. This matters for writing: nobody says a fish is
"badly made". They say the water was bad.

## The breach

**A breach is a place where the fabric is worn through far enough to see hyperspace.** In-universe
slang, and the mod's UI, also calls one a *rupture*; "pond" is the player-facing shorthand the
fishing trade uses and is fine in dialogue from anyone in the trade.

A breach is **not** a jump point. It is not a passage, it is a fault:

- it is not complete — it does not go anywhere, it merely stops being opaque;
- it is not stable;
- it will rend anything that tries to pass through it.

That last one is the whole of why the trade exists in the shape it does. You cannot go through. You
can only send something that does not mind being unmade, and get it back before the fabric closes on
it.

## Patterns

**The official term is *pattern*. "Fish" is slang**, used by ratings, dockworkers, buyers and
anybody who has not thought about it. The mod's UI uses "fish" almost everywhere, deliberately —
that is the register of the people using it. "Pattern" is what the Fisherman says, and what somebody
says when they are being precise and would rather not be.

A pattern is a **hyperspace ghost**. It cannot exist on this side, so on being pulled through it
takes a shape. For a reason nobody has explained, that shape is very often a fish.

Not always. It is also crabs, molluscs, and things the trade files under *other* because nothing
else fits. The pattern is not choosing a fish. Something about the crossing produces fish, and the
trade has stopped asking.

Two things follow that writing should honour:

- **A pattern is not an animal.** It has no biology, no life cycle, nothing under the skin that is
  there for a reason. When one comes apart, it does not die; it stops holding a shape. Descriptions
  in `fish.csv` should read like something *imitating* an animal with imperfect reference material.
- **The imitation is not for your benefit.** A pattern that looks back is not curious. The
  Fisherman: *"It is not the looking. It is that the thing looks back, and it does not know how to
  stop, and it does not know that you are not water."*

## The gear

The whole rig exists to reach through a breach without going through it yourself.

### ROD

**The ROD is the technology that lets something pass through a breach into hyperspace and come back.**
Everything else in the trade is a delivery system for a ROD head.

It cannot carry living beings. They come back inside out — the polite word is that they lose
coherence, and the polite word is doing a great deal of work.

**The ROD is not sanctioned.** The Church and the Path consider it heretical, for reasons that are
entirely obvious once stated and are therefore never stated. Everybody else considers it a safety
hazard at best. Nobody licenses it, nobody insures it, and a patrol that finds it on you has
opinions.

### LYNE drones

**LYNE drones** are the drones that pass through a breach on a ROD and pull patterns back. The
acronym has no expansion yet — nobody in the trade uses one, and if the mod ever needs the words
they should be dull, technical and slightly wrong.

They work from the drone side because a pattern cannot see something coming at it from a lower
dimension. That is the whole tactical premise of drone fishing: it does not know you are there.

The player-facing name in the UI is often just "drones" or "the rod". Both are fine.

### The harpoon

**A targeted, high-speed, retrievable shot carrying a ROD head**, to pierce a pattern and bring it
back through the breach. It is the aimed alternative to the drones' patience.

The **rope** is not a rope. It is the retrieval working, and the retrieval is not mechanical.
"Rope" is slang and the mod uses it in dialogue; the drawn line exists **for gameplay legibility
only** and should never be treated as a physical object in prose. Nothing is reeled. Nothing is tied.

### Breach lights

**Searchlights, properly breach lights.** They do not illuminate. They **weaken realspace wherever
they shine**, thinning the fabric until a ROD can pass through it — a breach made to order, where
there was none.

Shining them anywhere people are present is the worst thing in this mod that is not a war crime, and
only because nobody has got round to writing the law. Low coherence over a habitat, a station, a
fleet, does what low coherence does: it edits the people in it, without malice, without noise, and
without the possibility of appeal. Whole crews.

This is why every faction that polices anything treats running the lamps near a world the way they
would treat discharging a weapon into it — not as smuggling, as an attack that has not landed yet.

## The Fisherman

A mercenary captain. He worked with a scientist on the ROD after the gates fell, when everyone was
trying anything that might move a ship again.

He is not a man any more. Excessive exposure to low coherence — which is to say, standing next to
breaches, which is to say, doing the job — detached him from reality. He does not reliably conform
to the laws of realspace, and has not for a long time.

The registry has him as **Baha**. He does not use it, and says the paperwork never caught up and he
has never asked it to.

Write him:

- **Flat, unhurried, unbothered.** He is not menacing. He is not warning you. He answers questions,
  usually not the one you asked, and stops when he is done.
- **He does not perform.** No riddles, no portent, no capital-letter pronouncements.
- **He is helpful.** Genuinely. He gives you gear, work and charts, and there is no catch that
  anybody has found. That should be more unsettling than a catch would be.
- **He never explains what he is.** He does not appear to think it is interesting.

His **name degrades where the water is bad** — letters drop out by position, the same way in the same
place every time, because the degradation is a property of the place rather than of him.

### The crew

Equally gone, and equally untroubled. They trade places and faces. They reach through things. They
do things that should not work, with complete nonchalance. **None of them minds, and none of them
appears to notice.**

Written correctly, this is always incidental — a subordinate clause in a line about something else.
The lights sweep past the window and for the length of the sweep the crew are standing in a
different order. Nobody remarks on it, including the narration.

**The scientist is aboard.** He merged with the crew. He is there, and he is not. Whether any given
crewman is partly him is not a question the fiction answers.

### The fleet

A ghost fleet in the Flying Dutchman sense. It turns up where it should not be. It moves without
travelling. Nobody sees it, nobody cares about it, and it returns the compliment.

Mechanically the mod keeps one boat in every inhabited system and one that visits the frontier, and
they are all crewed by the same man. **This is not a franchise.** It is one entity that is in more
than one place, and the fiction should never resolve it further than "they do not explain how".

### Crablobab

A low-coherence entity that spawned off the Fisherman's fleet. He is the shape of a man in a coat,
and it is all wrong. The coat has far too many pockets and far more space than a coat has.

He is cheerful, talkative and sells things. He is named after his crabs, or they after him; he says
it saves time. He is not a threat and not a mystery to be solved — he is a man with a coat, and the
coat is the horror, and nobody is going to mention the coat.

## Fishing, and why anybody does it

Patterns kept appearing under breaches. Pulling them out was the next logical step.

They turned out to be **tasty — mostly**. That is the whole of the commercial case. The trade moves
them as food and as curiosities, and **the people buying them do not generally know where they come
from**.

Nothing in the fiction has ever established what eating one does. Nobody has asked in print.

## Register: who calls things what

| Thing | Trade / official | Slang, and the UI | Never |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reality's substrate | the fabric | the water | "the veil", "the aether" |
| A worn-through place | a breach, a rupture | a pond, a spot | a jump point, a portal, a gate |
| A hyperspace ghost | a pattern | a fish | a creature, an animal, a species (except as a table term) |
| The drones | LYNE drones | the drones, the rod | robots, probes |
| The retrieval line | — | the rope, the line | cable, cord, winch line |
| The lamps | breach lights | searchlights, the lights, the lamps | floodlights, spotlights |
| Local reality's condition | coherence | good water, bad water, thin water | "corruption", "taint" |

The mod's own UI leans slang almost everywhere, because the UI belongs to whoever is holding the
rod. Precision is a *character* choice — somebody being careful, or somebody who knows too much.

## Not yet decided

Held open deliberately. Do not invent answers to these in a `rules.csv` row; if a line needs one,
settle it here first.

- **What the Fisherman does with the catch he takes in trade.** The outfitter is bought with fish
  and he says there is no other currency out here. Nothing says why he wants them or what becomes
  of them.
- **What eating a pattern does**, if anything, and whether anybody has connected it to anything.
- **Why gates, hypershunts, collapsed stars, slipstreams and the abyss thin the fabric**, and why
  they rank the way they do.
- **Who buys low-coherence specimens, and for what.** The trade prices them differently and the mod
  says so out loud in a tooltip.
- **Where charts come from** — the trade sells survey data on where a given pattern lives, and
  nothing establishes how anybody surveys the other side of the fabric.
- **What comes up alongside the catch.** The rig recovers salvage, rare technology, blueprints and
  occasionally an entire hull. Nothing explains what those are doing there.
- **What a Breach Conservatory actually keeps.** A colony structure with a public display tank,
  stocked from the player's own catch, holding patterns alive indefinitely on this side.
- **What a pattern's rarity is a measure of**, and why a pattern cares about the local star's colour,
  its constellation's age or which part of the sector it is in.
- **Buried patterns** — the ones that never surfaced, visible only as a dent under a lamp.
- **What happens to a ship a ROD harpoon hits**, beyond the bill for it.
