# Catch.Release: The Needle Theorem

## Overview

- **Theme:** Quiet existential horror about the player’s freedom to reshape a world whose people have to live with the results.
- **Philosophy:** The Fisherman chooses to live within the world’s limits; the Scientist pursues knowledge without regard for its inhabitants. The player must decide what those lives are worth.
- **Content:** Fish measurements, legendary hunts and concealed experiments on the captain lead to a choice: take extraordinary power, or protect familiar people and places without an equivalent reward.

## Concept reference - 6 September 2026

**Status: deferred narrative brainstorming. Revisit quest design once the three fishing ships are finished.** This concept note preserves the author’s decisions and open options for that discussion. [LORE.md](LORE.md) remains the current lore authority; this note does not amend it or authorize quest implementation. Sections marked as directions or established concepts reflect the conversation’s decisions; suggested scenes, reward details and ending mechanics remain open where indicated.

**Preserve above everything else: the player’s perspective on the game becomes an object of the quest, while every important consequence remains grounded in characters and their lives.**

The player expects a game to accommodate their actions. Quests progress, improbable objectives become achievable, and rewards become available through effort. To the player this is ordinary play. To people living on the fabric, the accommodated world is their actual life.

The captain is the cross-section of the player’s needle on that fabric. The Fisherman is an in-universe mirror who has chosen to participate through ordinary work. He understands more than the inhabitants around him, but the player’s source extends beyond even his perspective. The Scientist is another expression of external will, narrowly assembled around investigation. He measures the anomalous captain without understanding what that outline belongs to.

His initially menial research becomes a concealed series of experiments on the player. These experiments increasingly force a record of the player’s influence into reality. At the central decision, the player discovers what they have been participating in and chooses between extraordinary power with destructive instability, or preserving the continued lives of people and places they have come to know.

## 1. The needle theorem

“Needle theorem” is author-facing shorthand. It is not a scientific proof or a named law that characters need to explain.

### Reality and accommodation

Imagine ordinary reality as a painted fabric. People, objects, records, observations and histories all belong to it. A needle pushed through from behind appears within the painting as a cross-section, although its extent exceeds what that painting can contain.

Reality accommodates the presence and actions of something entering from outside. Its inhabitants experience the resulting world as their world. Their current memories and records are part of that accommodation; they are not choosing to ignore an impossibility or participating in a conspiracy.

Ordinary people can encounter conflicting measurements and act on practical problems. That does not give them a broader perspective on the system supplying their continuity. Neutral narration reports what happens without announcing that reality has rewritten itself. The player can compare scenes.

### Outside is relative

The author’s central formulation:

> “The fisherman is the in-universe mirror to the player, but he has decided to play by the games rules”

The player acts from beyond the larger space in which the Fisherman and Scientist exist. To the player, that larger space-including hyperspace-is itself part of the played fabric.

| Level in the metaphor | What belongs to it | Relationship to what enters |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordinary fabric: 2D** | People, ships, objects, records and histories experienced from within. | External will can be expressed through forms reality accommodates. |
| **The larger space: 3D** | The Fisherman and Scientist have an extent beyond ordinary fabric. | The Fisherman understands that relationship directly. The Scientist’s assembled purpose remains confined to explaining the fabric. Neither contains the player’s full source in an account of their own world. |
| **The player: a further outside position** | Both lower levels belong to the game being played. | The player’s needle passes through both levels; its cross-section on ordinary fabric is the captain. |

The dimension numbers describe relationships between perspectives. They do not replace the setting’s spatial physics with literal two-dimensional geography.

As the author put it:

> “The player just happens to stick his needle through two dimensions.”

The Fisherman directly experiences what the player is, within the limits of his broader perspective. This is not eyesight, a scan or an inference assembled from instruments. The player’s source still extends beyond what he can understand whole.

The Scientist measures the outline on the fabric. It produces anomalous results, but he does not recognize it as the cross-section of a needle. His own external origin does not provide the Fisherman’s breadth of understanding.

### The captain and character creation

**The captain is the cross-section itself: the implement of the player’s will on the fabric.** The captain is not a separate human selected for possession. The player moves the captain, and the game’s reality accommodates that presence and those actions.

Character creation follows from the same relationship. The player specifies a name, appearance, capabilities and whatever past the selected start supplies. Reality presents a world in which that captain belongs. To the player, this is an expected function of the game. To its inhabitants, this person has a place in their world.

The captain can have a crew, relationships and a local history without being the independent source of the agency expressed through them. The player can remember an earlier account of events and act on information the current world no longer establishes.

That ability does not need to be acquired through a quest or turned into unrestricted magic. Ordinary decisions can introduce outside information: returning to a former entrance, asking after a person no current roster lists, or refusing a task because of something the player remembers.

## 2. Coherence and the expression of will

### Exposure changes the fabric, not the person’s dimensional status

**Exposure makes the patch of fabric a person inhabits less coherent and more susceptible to change.** It does not gradually promote crew members into higher-dimensional beings. The earlier wording about crew becoming “3D” through exposure has been superseded.

Less coherent fabric gives an external will greater opportunity to express itself through what is there. The Scientist can manifest through a current or former crew member because their circumstances are susceptible to change, not because they have advanced toward his nature.

Human bodies are not an absolute requirement. A sufficiently unstable rock could also become a vehicle for that expression. However, accommodating a will through something that reality does not already account for as having wants and intentions requires more “effort.” Humans are especially suitable because other people and the fabric already account for them as agents with purposes.

This is a property of what reality already accommodates. It is not a moral weakness, a contest of personal willpower or a special species-exclusive possession rule. The exact meaning of “effort” is not being formalized into a resource system.

### Forming and keeping a manifestation

- **Low coherence makes it easier for a needed form to manifest.**
- **High stability makes it easier for an established form to remain.**

The Professor can manifest a laboratory as circumstances permit. Its rooms, instruments and supporting arrangements accommodate his research. He need not understand that this is how the laboratory came to be; to him, its equipment can be ordinary infrastructure.

A place that was unstable and has become stable, or one that repeatedly passes between those conditions, is especially useful to him. A station moving through such conditions is a possible setting, not a claim that every station’s orbit naturally produces them.

This cycle gives consequences a recognizable sequence. A new arrangement becomes possible, then becomes established. Stability is not automatically a cure: it can preserve a harmful arrangement once that arrangement exists.

## 3. The Fisherman: a complete expression of will

The Fisherman and Scientist are both expressions of will. **The Fisherman is a complete expression, retaining the breadth of the human being he formerly was.** The Scientist expresses a narrower surviving purpose. The distinction is completeness, not merely whether an original human once existed behind either figure.

The Fisherman does not measure the player to discover their nature. He directly feels, experiences and understands the relationship through his broader perspective. Like the patterns, he does not literally see; unlike the patterns, he can understand. His apparent eyes and ordinary human behavior are not the mechanism of that understanding.

### His chosen life

He has chosen to play by the game’s rules: fish, make arrangements, teach methods, pay for work and enjoy the catch. He really lives there. His work, habits and attachments are sincere, even though his extent exceeds the world containing their local expression.

His commitment to keeping the world together is personal. He is not an appointed cosmic steward with authority over every event. He wants a world that continues holding for people, including himself, to live in. He can tolerate some alterations and risks the player might reject and continue enabling fishing despite its larger effects.

He has practical experience with beings like the player. This does not grant omniscience, immunity to low coherence or reliable prediction of this player’s decisions. Literal continuity across other users and saves remains unconfirmed in the story; familiarity can permit that inference without declaring that he remembers every campaign.

No additional invented past disaster or example of him misjudging another player is required. The earlier open question about his “specific prior experience” was only a possible invitation to supply an anecdote. The concept already has what it needs: experience with this kind of presence and limited control over how an individual responds.

### How he intervenes

At first he leaves the Scientist’s menial experiment alone because it has little impact. Intervention begins when the work changes direction and becomes dangerous.

He must never explain the needle theorem, the player’s nature or the background cosmology outright. He can give direct operational instructions and discuss immediate events. The larger realization belongs to the player.

He tries to prevent harm. When he cannot, he asks for genuine recovery work: retrieve a crew, return to an affected livelihood, keep a promise, preserve something the people using it value. He does not manufacture a crisis or sacrifice people as a lesson in empathy. The Scientist’s experiments create the danger; the Fisherman helps the player encounter its consequences.

He cannot make the player care and does not secretly know that they eventually will.

## 4. The Scientist / Professor: a purpose that keeps assembling itself

“Professor” describes the friendly questgiver persona; “Scientist” also refers to the surviving purpose behind it. No final name is assigned.

The Scientist’s interest in knowing persists more reliably than his personhood. An external will expresses itself through susceptible fabric, commonly through someone already accounted for as a person with intentions. That person may be a current or long-departed Fisherman crew member. The Professor can manifest outside the Fisherman’s fleet, with a laboratory as circumstances permit.

He is friendly, encouraging and materially helpful. His pleasure in the player’s accomplishments can be sincere. But another person’s welfare has no independent weight when it conflicts with the investigation. He wants to know, without a corresponding commitment to others knowing for their own sake. He will lie and arrange circumstances to obtain what he wants.

That limitation arises from the narrowness of the surviving expression, not a mental-illness explanation. He need not drop a pleasant act and become a different character when the cost is revealed. The relationship can have been real while its limits are terrible.

### Knowledge, perspective and continuous memory are different

The Scientist can possess useful facts, methods and intuitions without the Fisherman’s broad outside perspective. His purpose is to explore the fabric and explain reality in the context of the fabric. He cannot recognize the player as a needle by merely improving his measurements of the captain’s outline. He can also be unaware that he himself is an expression of hyperspace.

The latest clarification adds a specific memory rule:

| State | What the Scientist remembers and recognizes |
|---|---|
| **During one continuous manifestation** | He fully recognizes changes to reality that occur during it. He can remember a person or state that current records and other people no longer establish. He may deliberately lie about the difference. |
| **On changing host or re-manifesting** | The reality he manifests into becomes his current state and absolute truth. His new manifestation genuinely lacks memory of an earlier account that this reality no longer contains. |
| **Information present in the current reality** | Current research records and results remain available. They can preserve the programme’s progress across manifestations because they belong to the current world. They are not an archive immune to all changes. |

Recognizing that reality changed during an uninterrupted manifestation does not give him a complete account of his own origin or the player’s nature. This is a bounded continuity of recognition, distinct from the Fisherman’s broader understanding. Earlier wording that only the Fisherman could ever recognize a change is superseded for the Scientist under this rule.

### The assistant: deliberate deception becomes sincere denial

The Scientist has a research assistant. A task changes reality so that everyone else says he never had one. While the same manifestation persists, the Scientist remembers the assistant and makes an obvious excuse about their temporary absence-for example, that they went to fetch lunch. He is knowingly misleading the player.

After he changes host or re-manifests, he genuinely does not remember having an assistant. The new reality is his starting truth. The same denial has a different basis, which the player can recognize through the sequence of encounters.

The research can continue while the investigator’s personal memory of its harm is lost. His current test records still exist, but the person who helped produce them need not remain in the world’s account.

### The record of the player is the danger

Reality contains the player’s local appearance and the experimental results. The Scientist therefore retains access to those results on re-manifesting. By continuing the programme, he is **actively writing a persistent record of the player’s influence into reality even though the player is an outside needle, non-persistent within the fabric**. This creates the proposed paradox.

“Non-persistent” here concerns the player’s relationship to the fabric; it does not mean the real person playing has no continuity. The research attempts to give an outside influence a settled account within a world that cannot contain its source.

**A distinction still worth developing:** character creation and ordinary records already accommodate the captain’s local appearance. The new danger should concern the experimental attempt to give a fixed account of the agency and effects extending beyond that appearance. Otherwise, every port’s routine captain record would seem to create the same paradox. The exact threshold is not settled here.

## 5. The opening: an unstable fish and a measuring machine

The initial job is menial and has little impact: catch unstable fish and measure them with the provided machine. This is why the Fisherman leaves the work alone.

The author’s proposed sequence:

1. The player can measure their hand instead of the fish. The expectation is that ordinary player curiosity will make this attractive.
2. If they measure the fish, a later opportunity permits measuring their hand for fun.
3. If they still do not, the device is dropped onto their foot and measures it. The Scientist arranged this outcome.

The body measurement provides the anomalous result that redirects the Scientist toward what the captain is and can do. The player does not learn that significance at the time. His later requests continue to look like collection, fieldwork and equipment trials.

**Small causal detail to resolve:** if the Scientist has not yet discovered anything anomalous about the captain, his reason for arranging the foot measurement should not require advance knowledge of that anomaly. One option is that he wants a routine control sample and disregards the player’s reluctance; the unexpected result then changes his interest. Another is an earlier incidental reading. The author’s planned foot fallback remains recorded; the precise motive is open.

**Tone note:** keep the fallback brief and matter-of-fact. A long sequence of invitations and comic accidents would advertise the author’s insistence and undermine the intended subtlety. The fixed inciting measurement can happen through different behavior without presenting refusal as a promised escape from the entire plot.

## 6. Direction: disconnected side quests converging on a main quest

**The selected structure is a few disconnected side quests culminating in a central main quest with an overarching narrative.** A single six-stage exposition chain is not the selected starting structure.

Early work introduces ordinary people and supplies the resources needed for legendary fishing. A former crew member establishes a business; an outfitter needs specimens; someone maintains equipment. Their lives and the player’s involvement matter before they enter the larger struggle.

The Professor’s requests secretly test the captain’s capabilities. Assignments demand increasingly improbable results, eventually outcomes the existing circumstances cannot adequately support. The Scientist measures the achievement and its effect on reality. Consequences become increasingly noticeable and harmful.

Progress should involve more than harder enemies or rarer catches. After the player achieves something, the world can contain an account that accommodates a result its earlier circumstances did not support. The apparent task was one experiment; the player’s effect on the circumstances was another.

The player should remain unaware of being the concealed subject until the major discovery and decision. Earlier scenes can make retrospective sense without exposing the secret in advance. The fish and equipment trials can still be genuinely useful; the hidden purpose does not make all the work fake.

### Different viewpoints and a third person

The main narrative may be carried by the Scientist, Fisherman and an initially unaffected third party drawn into the struggle. These are possible changes of focal character or quest contact, not permission for neutral narration to explain the mechanism.

The third party may become the personal focus of the final decision. A female character, close friend or possible romantic attachment is an option, not a settled identity. They should have interests, work and decisions that matter before their survival becomes a stake. The player needs to care about the life being preserved rather than only recognize the category “friend” or “love interest.”

### Preparation quests and the three ships

Procedural catch requests can provide money, equipment and resources that make the legendary hunts reasonably achievable. Fixed conversations and recurring people give those jobs narrative continuity. Already-prepared players need not be forced through long supply grinds.

Three unimplemented fishing ships are available as rewards. Their allocation is unresolved. Earlier suggestions placed them after hunts two, four and six, but those milestones remain optional. The ships can support progression without being equivalent to the final reality-bending power offer.

## 7. Six legendary subjects - a provisional use

The six fish provide distinct phenomena that the Scientist can study while secretly studying the captain. The order and applications below are suggestions, not final chapters or metaphysical ingredients required by the universe.

| Legendary | Existing characteristic | Possible narrative use |
|---|---|---|
| **False Dawn** | A star-like sensor return resolves into a bright jellyfish-like shape; nearby lighting responds late. | Classification and measurement create a credible early research task after the initial body measurement. |
| **Lantern Jack** | An old but correctly formatted distress signal acts as a lure. | Test responses to recognizable signals whose apparent source is unreliable; a useful extra observation becomes dangerous. |
| **The Quorum** | Many fish register as one specimen even when divided. | Place the hunt near an impactful manifestation scene, allowing the player to connect identity with changing apparent embodiment. |
| **The Imposter** | A Fisherman boat’s profile and transponder resolve into a whale-shaped body while retaining the code. | Bring familiar identity into the investigation. A possible place for the Fisherman and Scientist’s first shared scene or the central discovery. |
| **Slipstream Moray** | Nearby slipstreams curve toward it and plotted courses update. | Show the relation between a subject and its surroundings; bring the laboratory’s changing conditions into the foreground. |
| **Abyssal Ghost Manta** | Its form has never finished becoming a manta. | A possible late subject for work on making unstable forms or results persist. Both allegiances can still have reasons to participate. |

These can develop a sequence of appearance, signal, identity, surroundings and persistence. They need not each deliver a corresponding lore explanation.

Existing-source consideration for later design: legendary catches are unique and permanent. Prior catches and retained observations should be recognized so that exploration does not invalidate chapters. This is a constraint to remember, not an implementation task in this note.

## 8. The discovery and allegiance break

The player must discover that they were being experimented on and understand the stakes before choosing to continue or stop. The discovery should arise through an action or investigation, with both contacts available as possible people to approach.

### Leading option: finding the sensors

The player uses their ordinary agency to discover sensor equipment recording their actions. They can bring it to the Scientist or Fisherman and ask about it. Its configuration or records can make earlier tasks legible as experiments on the captain.

The Scientist can acknowledge his methods and offer what the results make possible without explaining the player’s actual nature. The Fisherman can address consequences, ask for a practical intervention and direct attention toward affected people without delivering a cosmological lecture.

### Alternative: a Fisherman lead

The Fisherman gives a practical reason to inspect a location where the player can discover the equipment or its consequences. He leads them to evidence rather than supplying the theorem.

### More elaborate alternative: survival through a rupture

The player is pulled through a rupture and survives, exposing an exceptional relationship. This remains a higher-effort option. It would need to be handled consistently with the captain as the player’s cross-section, without implying that ordinary people can safely use retrieval as transport.

The sensor discovery currently offers the clearest connection between the player’s own investigation and the realization that they were already the subject. No discovery route has been finalized.

## 9. The choice: extraordinary power or continued existence

### Scientist route

The proposed reward is a choice of exceptionally powerful, deliberately “cheaty” benefits. Possible domains are combat, travel or fishing, with a pick-one structure. The selected benefit could make its domain almost effortless.

The exact item, capability, magnitude and presentation are undecided. The reward should deliver real power. The choice follows the discovery, so the player knowingly continues despite the instability and potential loss of a person, community or place they care about.

The Scientist can remain friendly and fulfill his promises. He does not need to attack the player, withhold payment or reveal a grand plan that he understands in full.

### Fisherman route

**No compensating power reward is required.** The reward is that the game’s world keeps holding and the friend, partner, planet or other valued life continues to exist. The player gives up the additional capability rather than receiving an equivalent safe version of it.

An itemless conclusion should still have an experienced aftermath. The preserved person can resume work, make a decision or continue a relationship. A community can continue the ordinary life the player helped establish. This is narrative consequence, not a substitute stat bonus.

The exact ending operation remains open: prevent a result from becoming established, dismantle a laboratory under suitable conditions, preserve a host’s life when the Professor fails to hold, or another practical intervention. Stabilization can preserve a harmful manifestation, so the sequence needs to respect the coherence rules.

### Editorial assessment of the reward direction

This is a strong fit for the premise. The player is choosing whether to gain more effortless control over the game at the cost of something within it. The Fisherman offers continued participation in a world with people and constraints worth accepting.

Three design concerns remain:

- **Make the benefit something players actually want.** If it only removes the part they most enjoy, refusing it is not a meaningful sacrifice. A pick-one offer can preserve the rest of the game while making one desired activity exceptionally easy.
- **Make the stake personal before making it endangered.** A gender or romance label cannot create the necessary attachment on its own. The person needs a life, choices and shared history with the player.
- **Make the tradeoff legible at the decision.** The reward can be powerful and the consequences severe, but an informed choice should not secretly substitute a punishment for the promised benefit.

The game need not declare that ordinary play is wrong. The player reaches this decision after experiencing what their participation has required of other lives.

## 10. Scene bank and tone

**Host changes are impactful plot events, not a recurring visual gimmick.** They explain the Scientist’s nature through selected encounters. Use them where they change the player’s understanding; avoid unnecessary repetitions. The target is subtle existential horror, consistent with the rest of the mod.

### A crew member leaves; the Professor returns

A normal crew member departs after a conspiratorial discussion, possibly to communicate with the Fisherman. The person present on return is the Professor. The reverse can occur when his manifestation fails and a prominent crew member resumes an ordinary concern.

### The assistant and the re-manifestation

The Scientist lies about the absent assistant while he still remembers them. After a later, impactful host change, he sincerely accepts the reality in which there was no assistant. Use this contrast to communicate the boundary of his continuity without an explanation.

### The former crew member’s independent life

The player helps a former crew member establish a livelihood. Later, the Professor occupies that life and its workshop serves his research. Someone’s ambitions have become the infrastructure of another purpose. The Professor is pleased to see the player and offers useful work.

### Scientist and Fisherman in the same room

Their first shared scene occurs before the player has been told they are connected. They anticipate each other’s objections, recognize equipment and respond to familiar habits. Their relationship becomes apparent through an immediate problem rather than a history lecture.

### The Professor in the player’s fleet

During exposure to enough unstable fabric or very low stability, the Scientist may manifest within the player’s fleet retinue. A person already accounted for in that setting offers a suitable expression of will. The scene should be used sparingly and retain an ordinary surface; it need not be staged as a flashy invasion or supernatural announcement.

### A wrong truth becomes established

In low stability, the Scientist says something close to a wrong truth. After the player leaves, the world supplies that account. Proposed scales include a planet or system name, appearance or colors, ownership, a fleet’s faction, or the identity of the place the player occupies.

One personal variant is an occupied laboratory annex subsequently accounted for as unmanned. The player knows people who used it. The Scientist’s interpretation of the result and the Fisherman’s attempted prevention give the event its character stakes.

The causal relation between definition, departure and stabilization remains open. The scene should belong to the recurring accommodation process rather than establishing that anything the Scientist says automatically becomes true.

### A reward fits the world

A reward or opportunity arrives as promised. The player initially recognizes normal gameplay, then encounters a familiar person whose circumstances now fit that arrangement. A small number of grounded examples can make ordinary convenience acquire another meaning.

Which additional gameplay expectations become evidence is still unanswered. Character creation and the tutorial skip are already established conceptual connections; the wider set should not be invented merely to fill a list.

## 11. Preserve these distinctions

| Distinction | Current direction |
|---|---|
| **Player / captain** | The captain is the cross-section of the player’s needle, not an independently originating host being possessed. |
| **Unstable fabric / transformed person** | Exposure lowers local coherence and permits change. It does not advance people toward being higher-dimensional. |
| **Human host / any possible expression** | Humans are especially easy because will and wants already fit the world’s account of them. A rock is possible with much greater accommodation. |
| **Fisherman / Scientist** | Both express will. The Fisherman is complete and broadly understands; the Scientist expresses a narrow investigative purpose. |
| **Understanding / eyesight or measurement** | The Fisherman experiences and understands directly. The Scientist measures the outline on the fabric. Neither the Fisherman nor patterns literally see as ordinary animals do. |
| **Within-manifestation memory / a new manifestation** | The Scientist recognizes changed reality while continuous. On re-manifesting, current reality becomes his truth. |
| **Research records / an immune archive** | Results carry forward because the present world contains them. This does not exempt the records from reality’s accommodation. |
| **Fisherman’s care / cosmic stewardship** | His care is personal and his ability limited. He has experience with beings like the player but cannot make this player care. |
| **Realization / exposition** | The player experiences consequences and compares scenes. The Fisherman does not deliver the background cosmology. |
| **Preservation / a balancing item reward** | The Fisherman route can give no extra power. Continued lives and a world worth inhabiting are its consequence. |

The pleasant trade, real rewards and ordinary relationships should remain convincing. They give the deeper interpretation something to act upon. The player’s benefits and attachments can both be sincere.

## 12. What remains open

- Which additional expectations of gameplay become concrete story evidence.
- The third party’s identity, independent goals and relationship with the player; friendship, romance and a female character are possibilities.
- The exact sensor discovery or alternative event that reveals the concealed experiments.
- The final power options, their domain and the concrete person, place or community endangered.
- The final intervention and how the stability cycle determines what can be preserved.
- The particular test that distinguishes recording the captain’s ordinary local appearance from forcing a persistent account of the outside agency into reality.
- The precise reason the Scientist arranges the foot measurement if the player refuses the hand option before an anomaly is known.
- Legendary ordering, how independent catches fit the story, procedural preparation volume and allocation of the three ships.
- The relationship between a dangerously precise description and the accommodation it provokes. A full physical law is not required, but recurring consequences should be intelligible.

The structure, opening device, manifestation-memory rule and policy of using host changes sparingly are no longer generic unanswered questions. A detailed backstory explaining a previous Fisherman mistake is not a prerequisite.

## The thought to recover later

The player expects a game to accommodate their will. The captain is the place that will appears on the fabric. The Fisherman understands this relationship from a broader but still limited position and has chosen a life within the world. The Scientist tries to explain the outline, accumulating results while losing memories of what earlier manifestations helped erase.

The investigation increasingly writes the player’s influence into a reality that cannot contain its source. The player eventually finds that their apparently ordinary questing was the experiment.

**The story asks what the player wants from this world after they have come to know the lives that accommodate their participation.** They may take extraordinary power, or choose to keep inhabiting a world whose people and constraints have become worth preserving. The theorem can remain unspoken while the choice makes its relationship felt.
